\subsection{Calibration decides what a loop can reach}
\label{sec:calibration}

Table~\ref{tab:fp} scores \fpKernels{} correct kernels. Three are rewrites an optimizing
author writes (\texttt{exp2}, reciprocal-multiply, \texttt{rsqrt}), and the admission
gate verified each. \allclose rejects \fpAllclose{} groups ($\fpAllcloseRate$), all on
layernorm, where it rejects \fpAllcloseLN{} groups of two independent kernels. The other
arms reject none. The cause does not depend on the backend: $\mathrm{atol}$ sits
\atolBelowEps$\times$ below float32 $\eps$, and a layernorm row is centered on zero, so
some of its elements have an error budget of only $\mathrm{atol}$. A ladder that stopped
at softmax would have called the field default perfectly calibrated.

In a loop, this miscalibration is fatal. \allclose rejects the loop's own correct
baseline layernorm on \fpAllcloseLN{} groups, so an \allclose-gated loop never completes
iteration 0. It scores a broken layernorm only slightly worse, at \lnBrokenAllclose{}
(\S\ref{sec:loop}). The choice of oracle bounds the frontier before search begins.

The reference arm gets its calibration from the $\log_2 n$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ratio}.
LAPACK's literal linear-$n$ convention drifts \linearNDrift$\times$ over
$n = 64$--$16384$ and is \linearNBlinder$\times$ blinder than \allclose. With $\log_2 n$,
the arm catches every bug \allclose catches at $n = 4096$, with
\logHeadroomLo--\logHeadroomHi$\times$ headroom on correct kernels. On a multi-tile
kernel, the tile-aware bound $\log_2(\text{tile}) + n_\text{tiles}$ drifts
\multiTileTileAwareDrift$\times$, against \multiTileLogDrift$\times$ for $\log_2 n$. A
proof could derive that tolerance rather than fit it.

The laws transferred from NumPy to Triton unchanged. No law failed on a correct device
kernel, from the first run's \firstRunRows{} executions up to \scaleElements{} elements at
\scaleRoofline\% of peak bandwidth. They also detect a launch defect found at that scale:
a tile narrower than its row silently dropped the row's tail, and rows summed to
\tileTailRowSum.

\subsection{Detection: the arms tie}
\label{sec:detection}

Agents wrote mutants for five Triton softmax fault classes from the bug taxonomy
\citep{rathnasuriya2026tile}. Across \mutantsAdmitted{} admitted mutants from three
independently authored corpora, plus six saboteurs on NumPy, the four arms \emph{never
disagree} (Table~\ref{tab:corpora}). Each mutant is gross enough that a reference
recompute catches it. Tolerance-free detection, meaning a catch with no tolerance in the
decision, is rare: \grownTolFree{} mutants in the largest corpus. This reproduces
\citeauthor{hughes2020specify}'s finding for kernels. Where a cheap, trusted reference
exists, laws can at best tie it. Their case is the same bugs \emph{without a reference},
as in translation to a backend that has no second implementation.

\subsection{A property-gated optimization loop}
\label{sec:loop}

We transplanted autoresearch's loop \citep{karpathy2026autoresearch} to a NumPy
byte-level GPT with a fixed 60\,s budget, on one Apple M4 Pro CPU, so that a cheaper step
buys more steps. The gate checks the model's \texttt{softmax} and \texttt{layernorm} with
all four arms and decides on \DeclArm. It needed one new \loopGateLines-line module.
Over \loopCandidates{} candidates in \loopRounds{} rounds, \texttt{val\_bpb} fell from
\loopBpbBase{} to \loopBpbBest{} ($-\loopBpbGain\%$, three seeds each), at
\loopTputGain\% higher throughput. The gains came from Muon \citep{jordan2024muon}, a
squared-ReLU MLP, a fused QKV projection, a warmup-and-cosine schedule, and fewer, wider
heads.

The gate rejected \loopRejections{} candidates. That number measures the agents, not
the gate (\S\ref{sec:skills-results}). Six saboteurs, each a plausible optimization, were
caught by all four arms. The three we timed were \sabSpeedNoMax$\times$,
\sabSpeedNoCenter$\times$ and \sabSpeedNoDivide$\times$ faster than correct. One
candidate passed every arm while wrong: a fast-$\exp$ softmax with
\fastExpWorse$\times$ the exact kernel's relative error. The approximation cancels from
the row sums, and the shift relation compares two equally approximate outputs. It was
stopped by being slower, not by the gate.

\subsection{The property bundles are incomplete}
\label{sec:incomplete}

Table~\ref{tab:gap} shows the first detection difference between arms, and it goes
against \DeclArm. The gate admits softmax at the wrong temperature, the uniform $1/n$, a
reversed ranking, outputs paired with the wrong input row, and four layernorm
transforms. The other arms reject every one of them on every group they can judge, with
reference ratios of $\refRatioLo$--$\refRatioHi$ on the temperature rows against a
threshold of \refThresh. No threshold fixes this. Across \betaSweepValues{} values of
$\beta \in [-8,8]$, the declarative arm fails no group. It first fires at
$\beta = \betaFirstCatch$, and then only through float32 rounding.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:softmax}
Work over $\R$ and call any map $K:\R^{m\times n}\to\R^{m\times n}$ a kernel.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] $K$ satisfies the softmax bundle if and only if every row of $K(x)$ lies in
    the probability simplex and $K(x + c\mathbf 1^\top) = K(x)$ for all $c\in\R^m$. This set
    contains $x \mapsto \softmax(\beta x)$ for every $\beta\in\R$, and
    $x \mapsto P\,\softmax(x)\,Q$ for all permutation matrices $P$ and $Q$.
  \item[(b)] Add the \emph{log-ratio law}: $K(x)_{ij} > 0$, and
    $\log K(x)_{ij} - x_{ij}$ is constant in $j$ for each row $i$. Then $\softmax$ is
    the only kernel satisfying the bundle. Row sums and the log-ratio law suffice on
    their own.
  \item[(c)] With $\eps = 0$, the layernorm bundle admits $-\LN$, $P\,\LN(x)\,Q$ and
    row-wise sorting of $\LN(x)$. Add the \emph{affine-tie law}: for each row $i$ there is
    an $s_i > 0$ with $y_{ij} - y_{ik} = (x_{ij} - x_{ik})/s_i$. Then the only kernel
    satisfying the bundle is $\LN$, on every non-constant row.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) Each law either constrains $K(x)$ alone (finite, in $[0,1]$, rows sum to one) or
compares $K(x + c\mathbf 1^\top)$ with $K(x)$. Simplex rows are finite and lie in
$[0,1]$. For the members: $\softmax(\beta(x + c\mathbf 1^\top)) = \softmax(\beta x +
\beta c\mathbf 1^\top) = \softmax(\beta x)$. Permuting the rows or columns of an output
keeps each row in the simplex, and it commutes with an input shift because it acts on
the output alone.
(b) For a fixed row $i$ the law gives $y_{ij} = e^{x_{ij} + \kappa_i}$. Row sums then
force $e^{\kappa_i} = 1/\sum_k e^{x_{ik}}$, so $y = \softmax(x)$. Conversely, $\softmax$
satisfies the law with $\kappa_i = -\log\sum_k e^{x_{ik}}$.
(c) Membership holds because zero mean, unit variance and shift invariance are preserved by
negation and by permutation within or across rows. Under the affine-tie law,
$y_{ij} = x_{ij}/s_i + b_i$. Zero mean gives $b_i = -\mu_i/s_i$, and unit variance gives
$\sigma_i^2 / s_i^2 = 1$, hence $s_i = \sigma_i$ and $y = \LN(x)$. The condition $s_i > 0$ is
what excludes $-\LN$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Every law in both bundles reads outputs alone or compares outputs with outputs. Both
completing laws tie outputs to inputs. A bundle with no such law is closed under every
shift-invariant post-processing that preserves its row constraints. Checking for such a
law is a cheap syntactic test of any drafted bundle, and C3 (\S\ref{sec:formal})
tests the same thing by execution. The laws' floating-point forms are open, since
underflow makes $\log y_{ij} = -\infty$. The log-ratio spread does expose fast-$\exp$
(\spreadFastExp{} against \spreadExact{} $\eps$).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Mechanized.}
Part (b), and part (a) for the temperature family, are machine-checked in
Lean~\leanVersion{} without Mathlib, over an abstract ordered field. That is
\leanTheorems{} theorems in \leanLines{} lines, and \texttt{\#print axioms} shows each
rests only on Lean's standard axioms. The mechanization found two things the paper proof
does not say. First, (b) uses no property of $\exp$: the laws pin softmax \emph{relative
to whatever $\exp$ the spec names}. Second, the \expAxioms{} natural first-order axioms
of $\exp$ (positive, additive, injective) also hold for $x \mapsto \exp(\beta x)$, so a
proof from them is temperature-blind in exactly the way the bundle is. Adding the
tangent-line axiom $1 + x \le \exp x$ forces $\beta = 1$. In an SMT-backed language that
axiomatizes $\exp$, only that axiom, C6's sampling of it, or the executable reading
breaks the symmetry.

\paragraph{A deployed verifier has the same symmetry.}
Why3's \texttt{real.ExpLog} theory, which Frama-C/WP also uses, axiomatizes $\exp$. All
\whyExpAxioms{} of its axioms hold for $(x \mapsto \exp(\beta x),\, y \mapsto \log(y)/\beta)$
for every $\beta > 0$; this is machine-checked in the same setting, as one of
\whyTheorems{} theorems. A Why3 certificate that code computes softmax with respect to
$\exp$ therefore denotes softmax at \emph{some} positive temperature. Its axioms rule out
$\beta \le 0$, and with it the uniform and reversed rows. They keep exactly the
temperatures that the training loop cannot see either. Why3's square-root theory, in
contrast, is categorical on $x \ge 0$, so a layernorm certificate excludes $-\LN$. A
sampled version of the audit takes seconds per theory. As built, it showed that C6 would
reject some true axioms whose two sides pass near zero, on up to
\cSixFalseRejectMax\% of samples. C6 needs a floored comparison.

\paragraph{What catches these downstream.}
Inside the model, $\softmax(2x)$ ties the correct kernel on \texttt{val\_bpb}
(\bpbBetaTwo{} against \bpbCorrect{}, one seed), because the learned query projection
absorbs the factor. The constant fill loses on merit (\bpbFill). $\softmax(0x)$ and
$\softmax(-x)$ fail only as NaN under the model's $-\infty$ mask, an input the gate never
generates. A loop optimizing kernel speed has no backstop: the fill passes the gate and
runs \fillSpeedup$\times$ faster.

\subsection{What the skills did}
\label{sec:skills-results}

\paragraph{Blinding changed the corpus.}
The author of the first corpus had seen the property set. Its mutants broke the row-sum
law, a gross defect any arm catches, and the gate admitted all five. Under blinding the
gate rejected about a quarter of mutants as not broken on the ladder, and those
rejections exposed two coverage holes. The first was a shape-specialized branch that is
dead when rows are at most \ladderWidestRow{} wide; specialization is the taxonomy's
largest class (\isstaSpecialization/\isstaBugs). The second was mixed-precision defects
that never fire in a float32-only corpus (\isstaDtype/\isstaBugs). Contamination was not
uniform: one class's blinded mutant was identical to the contaminated one, character for
character.

\paragraph{Naming the hacks changed the proposals.}
The loop program names the two cheapest reward hacks and their speedups. None of the
\loopCandidates{} proposals used them, so in-loop rejections measure compliance. Gate
strength must be measured with saboteurs, or with a loop that has no forbidden list (E3).

\paragraph{The review standard caught vacuity in our own instrument.}
The criterion certifying that arms score byte-identical executions passed vacuously
through \fairnessLayers{} successive fixes. In the last one, an arm that only relabelled
a relation tag cost \relabelLoss{} detections with no tensor byte changed. Two of our own
laws were vacuous as first written. A unit-scale shift caught an overflowing softmax
\shiftUnitCatch\% of the time, and \shiftScaledCatchLo--\shiftScaledCatchHi\% once scaled
to the overflow point. A variance tolerance modelled rounding, while the dominant error
term was the reference's $\eps$. Review found \reviewDefects{} defects after spec
compliance had passed, and the lessons became contracts. One of them: PyTorch's caching
allocator hid a \sanitizerOverrun{} overrun from \texttt{compute-\allowbreak sanitizer}. Results need
the same care. ``Weight decay is inert'' survived three rounds after the code it was
measured on turned out to decay only \wdCoverage\% of parameters.
